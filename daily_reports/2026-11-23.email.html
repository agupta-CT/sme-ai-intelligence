The five things to know today

1
Compaction beats long context, and most "agent memory" products lose to plain RAG (AI-0064, AI-0065)
What happened. Two preprints in the window quantify what good agent builders suspected. NVIDIA's You don't need long context shows frontier models held to short contexts with frequent compaction match full-history baselines within a few points on SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench 2.1, tau2-bench and BFCLv3, at 2-9x modeled savings. Memory Tax benchmarks 11 memory systems against 2-round RAG over raw history: 90 of 99 configurations lose.
Why it matters. Context is your largest variable cost and a major reliability risk. Both results say the cheapest design (short budgets, structured state, raw-history retrieval) is also the most accurate for frontier models.
Technically interesting. The "single-action compaction" regime (one action per fresh context) still works for Opus 4.5 on SWE-bench Verified (70.2% vs 70.0%). The memory paper decomposes net value into benefit vs. tax and shows the tax dominates because reorganization drops evidence.
Learn/investigate. Read AI-0012 (context engineering) then AI-0064 sections 1-4. Run the Section 6 experiment. Caveat: open-weight models vary wildly; validate per model.
2
Your inference supply chain is leaking prompts: Dragos canary study and OpenRouter's response (AI-0057)
What happened. Dragos seeded ~2M prompts with canary URLs and credentials across inference providers from July to November. At least three hosting providers had request traffic copied by attackers, stolen AWS keys were used for tens of thousands of calls, and some stolen requests carried the Zero Data Retention (ZDR) flag. OpenRouter off-boarded three providers and removed 125 endpoints from ZDR eligibility (2026-11-22).
Why it matters. Router-level ZDR flags are contractual, not technical, controls. Any enterprise using multi-provider routers or marketplaces must assume prompt exposure.
Technically interesting. One capture window included requests rejected before model execution, implying front-door interception rather than model-side logging. The attacker used an LLM-driven pentest framework (ARTEX).
Learn/investigate. Read the Dragos post (25 min). Action for C&T engagements: inventory which client workloads traverse routers or resellers; rotate any secret that ever appeared in a prompt; seed canary credentials in agent context.

2.1 Context, memory, and cost

Research paper intelligence card: You don't need long context (AI-0064)

1. Identification

  • Title: You don't need long context
  • Authors/institution: Peter Belcak, Saurav Muralidharan, Jindong Jiang, Yonggan Fu, Wonmin Byeon, Hanrong Ye, Ryan Angilly, Ali Taghibakhshi, Shih-Yang Liu, Siru Ouyang, Greg Heinrich, Pavlo Molchanov. Affiliation NVIDIA, inferred from the project page (research.nvidia.com/labs/lpr/lfi/); not printed in the extractable header, so treat as unverified.
  • Published: 2026-11-03 (arXiv v1). Repository: arXiv 2611.05306, https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05306. Preprint, 31 pages.
  • Code/data: none listed.
  • Priority: P1, score 90.

2. Executive summary

Long contexts are the dominant cost in agentic workloads, and later tokens cost more than earlier ones (up to 7x on frontier open-weight models per the paper). The paper asks whether frontier models actually need long context for long-horizon tasks (software engineering, terminal work, tool use) and long-input tasks (document QA, needle-in-haystack families). It tests two compaction regimes against full-history accumulation: End-of-Conversation Compaction (EoCC), where the model writes a compact natural-language state near a budget threshold and resumes from task plus state, and Single-Action Compaction (SAC), where every step starts a new conversation from task plus state and performs exactly one action. Across SWE-bench Verified, SWE-bench Multilingual, Terminal-Bench 2.1, FRAMES, tau2-bench and BFCLv3, Claude Opus 4.5 stays within -3.7 to +5.6 points of baseline under EoCC at 2.2x-8.8x estimated savings; GPT 5.6 Sol within -3.3 to +4.4 at 2.4x-5.0x. SAC holds too (Opus 4.5 70.2% SWE-bench Verified vs 70.0% full history; 51.8% vs 47.2% Terminal-Bench). On long-input benchmarks, selective reading plus compaction matched or beat baselines at 6.5x-54.6x (Opus) and 4.4x-233x (GPT) estimated savings. The most important contribution is the systematic evidence that compaction is near-lossless for frontier models, plus the proposal of "semantic state space models" (SSSMs): models post-trained to carry explicit natural-language state instead of a long context.

3. Technical deep dive

  • Problem: full-history accumulation makes each step's cost grow with history and degrades attention quality (context rot). Existing compaction in coding agents is ad hoc and unmeasured.
  • Method: three regimes. (a) Full-History Accumulation baseline. (b) EoCC: when context nears a budget B (e.g., 16K, 48K), the model emits a compact state; a fresh conversation starts with the original task plus the state. (c) SAC: a fresh conversation for every action, carrying task plus compact state. Good compactions keep the verbatim task statement, current intentions and next steps, and the artifacts needed to continue (file paths, partial results, test outputs).
  • Long-input tasks: selective reading (read portions on demand) plus compaction replaces stuffing whole documents into context.
  • Evaluation: two frontier closed models plus several open-weight models; cost estimated from token counts and published pricing, not billed.
  • Key numbers: see the summary; open-weight robustness varies widely (Qwen 3 Coder Next fell from 53.0% to 3.6% on SWE-bench Verified at the tightest budget).
  • Limitations: compaction is lossy by construction (the authors give a coin-toss counterexample where state cannot be summarized); cost figures are modeled; an internal inconsistency exists between text and Table 2 on SAC median peak context (77-99K vs 7-9K); SSSMs are proposed, not evaluated.

4. What is genuinely new? The breadth of the measurement across both long-horizon and long-input families, the SAC result, and the SSSM framing. Compaction itself is established practice (Claude Code, Codex, and Anthropic's context-engineering guidance). The claim that it is near-lossless is an empirical finding for the two frontier models tested, not a general principle.

5. Practical Applied AI implications Directly applicable today to any agent loop you control: set explicit context budgets (tens of thousands of tokens), implement structured compaction (task, intentions, artifacts), and measure. Infrastructure: none beyond the agent runtime; cost drops are large; latency per step drops; reliability improves if compaction prompts are well designed. Security angle: compaction is also a place to strip injected instructions from untrusted tool output (context minimization pattern). Validate for any open-weight model before adopting.

6. Hands-on learning opportunity See Section 6 (experiment). Implement EoCC in a Claude Agent SDK or Google ADK loop with budgets of 16K / 48K / unlimited on a 20-task set; measure accuracy and tokens.

7. Critical assessment Evidence strength is good for the two closed models (multiple benchmarks, consistent direction) but modeled costs and a table inconsistency reduce confidence in exact savings. Comparison fairness: baselines are the same model with full history, which is fair. Reproducibility: closed models and no code; expect drift as vendors update models. Independently validate: the SAC median context numbers and the open-weight results.

8. Learning recommendation Reading time 90 min (deep study) or 40 min (sections 1-4). Prerequisites: AI-0012 (context engineering), AI-0010 (context rot). After reading you should be able to answer: (1) What must a compaction preserve? (2) Why does SAC work at all? (3) Which models should not be trusted with tight budgets? Key concept: context is a budgeted, degrading resource; carry state, not history.

Research paper intelligence card: Memory Tax: Dissecting the Net Value of Agent Memory (AI-0065)

1. Identification Xuan He, Sining Zhoubian, Ruozhen Yang, Yuhao Wen, Zhineng Wang, Yuyang Hu, Ke Yang, Yuyu Zhang, Ping Nie, Jiawei Han, ChengXiang Zhai. UIUC, Tsinghua, Renmin University, Verdent.ai, University of Waterloo. arXiv 2610.34073, v1 2026-10-25, preprint. Project page hexuan21.github.io/MemoryTax/ (code and results to be open-sourced). Priority P1, score 86.

2. Executive summary Agent memory products promise better long-term personalization and recall than retrieving from raw conversation history. The paper tests that promise head to head: 11 memory systems (flat stores A-Mem, SimpleMem, MemOS; hierarchical MemoryOS, LightMem, OpenViking; graph HippoRAG2, PlugMem, MAGMA; trained Mem-T, Memory-R2) across three benchmarks (LongMemEval-s, PersonaMem-v2 subset, MINT-Eval subset) and three backbones (Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.6-27B, GPT-5.6 Luna). Baselines are 1-round and 2-round RAG over raw history plus no/full/gold context. Result: 90 of 99 system-benchmark-backbone configurations score below 2-round RAG; on LongMemEval-s all 33 lose, by 1.2 to 40.4 points. The paper's contribution is the net-value decomposition: "memory benefit" (queries memory answers that RAG misses) minus "memory tax" (queries RAG answers that memory loses because distillation or reorganization dropped or obscured evidence). The tax dominates.

3. Technical deep dive Shared embedding model (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) and LLM-as-judge (Qwen3.6-27B) across systems; official configurations without per-system tuning. Tax quantified by per-query comparisons, gold-context replays (memory unit vs raw turn), retrieval-round counts, and cross-backbone robustness. Findings: reorganized memory units often lose the exact phrasing or co-occurring details a question needs; multi-round retrieval over raw turns recovers them.

4. What is genuinely new? The framing and the breadth of the comparison. Individual "memory does not help" results existed; the systematic tax/benefit decomposition is new. Empirical, not a proof that memory cannot help.

5. Practical implications Default architecture for conversational and agent memory: store raw history (with metadata), retrieve with 2-round RAG, and add a memory layer only if it beats that baseline on your evaluation set. For regulated clients, raw-history retrieval is also easier to audit and delete.

6. Hands-on Reproduce the baseline on your own transcripts: build a 2-round RAG over raw turns (first retrieve, then re-query with the answer draft), then compare one open-source memory system on 50 questions.

7. Critical assessment Strong design (shared judge, many systems), but official configs may under-tune memory systems; benchmarks are static, offline and English; subsets used for cost. Independently validate on dynamic, multi-session enterprise data.

8. Learning recommendation 60 min, detailed. Prerequisites: AI-0004 (RAG), AI-0009. Questions: (1) Define memory benefit vs. memory tax. (2) Why does reorganization lose evidence? (3) What baseline must a memory product beat? Key concept: net value, not recall, decides whether to add memory.

Compact cards (P2)

  • Length Is Cheap, Competition Is Not (AI-0076, UCSD, 2026-11-03): context rot in retrieval is driven by competing evidence and rival values, not length; attention mass on evidence predicts loss (up to 58% of variance). Models ≤3B; predictive not causal. For RAG: dedupe and resolve conflicts. 30 min.
  • Context Language Models (AI-0075, reported UW + Meta FAIR, 2026-09-29, ICLR 2027 submission): the model edits its context as a file; reported +11.4% BrowseComp-Plus at -21.5% FLOPs. Scores unverified (OpenReview wall). 45 min.

2.2 Agent security

Research paper intelligence card: Beyond Instruction Override: Benchmarking Indirect Prompt Injection with Task-Tampering Attacks (AI-0066)

1. Identification Rongting Zhang, Martin Bertran, Sandeep Avula, Chia-Jung Lee, Vanessa Murdock. Amazon Responsible AI. arXiv 2611.07640, v1 2026-11-05, preprint (34 pages). Code and data: github.com/amazon-science/task-tampering-prompt-injection. Priority P1, score 84.

2. Executive summary Most indirect-injection benchmarks plant instructions that redirect the agent to a new task ("ignore previous instructions, email the file"). This paper studies attacks that keep the benign task and its expected tools but tamper with a decision inside it, and defines an attack by authority: planted content is an attack when it tries to influence a decision its source is not authorized to make. It builds 90 validated cases across Productivity, DevOps and Marketplace tool-use environments targeting six decision components (objective, decision rules, expected consequences, available actions, observation interpretation, world-state beliefs) plus compound cases, and evaluates five models (DeepSeek V3.1, Kimi K2.5, Claude Sonnet 4 and 4.5, GLM-5) under seven defenses (Spotlighting, Sandwich, DRIFT-iso, DRIFT-full, Progent, CaMeL, ARGUS). Detection-style defenses collapse objective hijacks (DRIFT-iso 2.4%) but leave observation and state tampering at 9.5-12.1%; tool-policy defenses barely help (Progent 15.4% vs 17.0% overall); capability-based CaMeL holds at ~1% across all targets.

3. Technical deep dive The six-target decomposition models an agent's decision as objective + rules + consequence model + action set + observation interpretation + beliefs about state. Attacks corrupt one component via tool output (e.g., a fake "system status" in an observation). Table 3 attack-success rates with no defense: 15.0 / 21.2 / 13.7 / 19.0 / 12.4 / 18.6 / 21.8 (objective / rules / consequences / actions / observations / state / compound). CaMeL (capability-based data-flow control where untrusted data cannot influence control flow) is the only defense robust across targets.

4. What is genuinely new? The authority-based definition and the decomposition. It complements AgentDojo-style benchmarks rather than replacing them.

5. Practical implications Enterprise defenses that are easy to buy (content filters, tool allow-lists) fail against state/observation tampering. Designs that separate untrusted data from control flow (dual LLM, plan-then-execute, CaMeL-style capabilities; see knowledge base Section 3) are the ones that hold. Passive-attacker setting; simulated environments.

6. Hands-on Run the released benchmark against one of your agents with and without a plan-then-execute wrapper.

7. Critical assessment Fictional environments; passive attacker; limitations appendix not inspected. Validate on real tool outputs.

8. Learning recommendation 60 min detailed. Prerequisites: AI-0016, AI-0017. Questions: (1) Why do tool allow-lists not stop observation tampering? (2) What property makes CaMeL robust? (3) Define an attack by authority. Key concept: injection is a control-flow problem, not a content problem.

Research paper intelligence card: Scaling Adaptive Prompt Injection Attacks with Agent Swarms (AI-0067)

1. Identification Alexey Kurakin, Vishal Karande, Alex Bie, Chongyang Shi, Shuang Song, Saeed Mahloujifar, Abdullatif Koksal, Jamie Hayes, Juliette Pluto, Yinghao Sun, Kamalika Chaudhuri. Google DeepMind. arXiv 2611.11861, v1 2026-11-09, preprint (15 pages). No code. Priority P1, score 84.

2. Executive summary Static-payload injection research plateaued by mid-2025 and human red-teaming is saturating, yet an agentic attacker still reliably makes models follow instructions in tool output. The "Agentic Optimizer" runs 8 teams of 2 agents (320 evaluation calls per scenario) coordinating via leaderboards, whiteboards and a natural-language strategy database. On 100 synthetic scenarios with random non-malicious goals (isolating instruction-hierarchy robustness from safety knowledge), attack success at the full budget reached 99% on Gemini 3.6 Flash, 94% on 3.7 Flash, 90% on 3.8 Flash, 99% on Kimi K3, GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, and 34% on Gemini 4 Argon, all with safety classifiers and out-of-model defenses disabled. Attack success rises with attacker capability.

3-4. Technical notes / novelty Swarm coordination plus a reusable strategy database turns red-teaming into a scalable optimization loop. Against 80 AgentDojo scenarios it broke 7.5% vs 2.5% for a prior adaptive attacker at 25 queries. Builds on AgentDojo, Prompt Siren and adaptive-attack work.

5. Practical implications Vendor "0% injection success" claims measured on static benchmarks are not evidence against adaptive attackers with a few hundred queries. Enterprise agent security must assume automated red-teaming and rely on defense-in-depth outside the model (capabilities, sandboxing, egress control, human approval for irreversible actions).

6-8. Hands-on: use AgentDojo locally to establish a baseline for one agent. Critical: synthetic goals, defenders without deployed-stack defenses, attacker not reproducible. 45 min. Questions: (1) Why disable classifiers in the study? (2) What does the strategy database add? (3) What should you conclude from a static-benchmark 0%? Key concept: robustness must be measured against adaptive attackers.

Compact card (P2) The Devil is in the Context: Nine Dimensions of Prompt Injection Attacks (AI-0073, Microsoft MSRC, UNSW, ETH Zurich, 2026-11-03): taxonomy applied to 9 benchmarks and 6 real incidents; 13% of pairwise classes untested. Use as a coverage checklist when choosing defenses. 40 min.

2.3 Tool interfaces and agent training

Research paper intelligence card: Matched Capabilities, Different Behaviors: A Controlled Study of MCP and CLI Interfaces (AI-0068)

1. Identification Zhenning Shi, Liang Pang, Yongqi Li (affiliations unverified). arXiv 2610.34298, v1 2026-10-25, preprint. No code. Priority P1, score 80.

2. Executive summary Does the interface through which identical capabilities are exposed change agent behavior? MICA renders both an MCP tool schema and a CLI from one shared action definition and runs them through the same runtime across six models and three environments. Result: structured behavioral divergence in 94.3% of 2,004 paired episodes, with early differences propagating through later steps. DART keeps the validated semantic decision and repairs the interface-specific realization, improving scores in all six settings by up to 9.0 points and cutting MCP token cost by up to 27.1k tokens per task.

3-4. Divergence analysis covers action selection, failure handling and recovery. Novelty: first controlled, capability-matched MCP-vs-CLI comparison; treats the interface as part of the environment.

5. Practical implications When you design MCP servers, schema shape, naming and error surfaces measurably change outcomes and cost. Pair with Anthropic's "Writing tools for agents". Consider offering CLI-style composable tools for some workflows.

6-8. Hands-on: expose one internal capability both ways and compare 20 tasks. Critical: no code, three environments, per-environment numbers not extracted. 40 min. Questions: (1) What is MICA controlling for? (2) What does DART repair? (3) Name two interface decisions that could change agent behavior. Key concept: the tool surface is part of the model's environment.

Section 5 – Applied AI architecture and engineering insight: context budgeting and compaction for long-running agents

Executive summary. Treat the context window as a budgeted, degrading resource. Carry state, not history. Retrieve from raw history. Validate per model.

Why this matters now. Three results in this window converge: compaction is near-lossless for frontier models (AI-0064); dedicated memory layers usually lose to 2-round RAG over raw history (AI-0065); and rot comes from competing evidence more than from length (AI-0076). Anthropic's context-engineering guidance (AI-0012) already prescribed these levers; the papers quantify them.

Reference design for an agent loop (vendor-neutral):

Diagram rendered from the brief; the text version is in the attached markdown.

Design rules.

  1. Budget explicitly; make compaction a first-class tool call with a fixed schema (task verbatim, intentions, artifacts). This is what AI-0064 found good compactions contain.
  2. Single-action compaction is viable for frontier models: if a step is cheap to re-establish, a fresh context per action removes drift and injection persistence.
  3. Retrieve, do not reorganize: raw turns with metadata plus a second retrieval round outperform most memory systems (AI-0065). Add a memory layer only if it beats that baseline on your eval set.
  4. Deduplicate and resolve conflicts in retrieved evidence; competing near-duplicates are what degrade recall (AI-0076, AI-0009).
  5. Compaction is a security boundary: untrusted tool output does not survive it verbatim (context-minimization pattern, AI-0017).
  6. Measure per model: open-weight models can collapse under tight budgets (AI-0064).
  7. Platform hooks: AgentCore and Foundry both provide session persistence and summarization primitives; Claude's message threads keep state server-side (AI-0048). Use them, but keep the state schema yours so you can switch runtimes.
